# Project: IRDAI Compliance Document Crawler

## Overview

This project aims to build an **automated document collection pipeline** for an Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system focused on **IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India)** regulations.

Instead of manually downloading regulatory documents, the crawler will automatically discover, download, organize, and maintain the latest IRDAI compliance documents from the official IRDAI website.

The downloaded documents will later be cleaned, chunked, embedded, and indexed in a vector database for use in an Agentic RAG system that answers insurance regulatory and compliance questions.

---

# Objectives

The crawler should:

* Crawl the official IRDAI website.
* Automatically discover pages containing regulatory documents.
* Identify downloadable PDF documents.
* Download only authentic IRDAI PDFs.
* Avoid duplicate downloads.
* Maintain metadata for every document.
* Support incremental updates when new regulations are published.

The crawler should prioritize high-value compliance documents rather than downloading every file on the website.

---

# Documents to Collect

The crawler should prioritize collecting documents such as:

* IRDAI Regulations
* Master Circulars
* Guidelines
* Circulars
* Acts
* Exposure Drafts
* Frequently Asked Questions (optional)
* Discussion Papers (optional)

The crawler should ignore unrelated content such as:

* Recruitment notices
* Tenders
* Annual reports
* Press releases
* Speeches
* News articles
* Login portals
* Consumer grievance portals
* Administrative webpages

* Some important note:

Yes, it is absolutely possible to clean out Hindi text word-by-word or sentence-by-sentence, but you should not do it by modifying the PDF files themselves.

Here is why, and what the correct solution is:

The Problem with PDF Modification
PDFs are not like Word documents; they don't understand "sentences" or "paragraphs." A PDF just knows to place a specific letter at a specific X/Y coordinate on the page. If you try to delete a Hindi sentence from a PDF programmatically, the English text around it will not automatically slide up to fill the gap. You would just be left with weird, empty white spaces in the middle of your document, and it can often corrupt the PDF's internal structure or reading order.

The Best Solution: Clean During RAG Ingestion
Since you are building a RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) system, you don't actually need the raw PDF files to be 100% perfectly clean. You only need the text that goes into your database to be clean.

The standard way to handle this is during your Data Ingestion / Chunking pipeline (the step where you read the PDF and prepare it for your vector database):

Extract all text from the PDF page.
Chunk the text into sentences or paragraphs.
Filter the chunks: Before sending a chunk to your embedding model, check it for Hindi text.
You can use the exact same regex we use in the scraper ([\u0900-\u097F]) to check if a sentence contains Devanagari characters.
If a sentence/chunk contains Devanagari, simply discard that chunk and don't add it to your vector database.
Keep the chunks that are purely English.
Why this is better:

It is incredibly easy to do in Python (just a simple if statement when processing your text).
It safely ignores the Hindi text without destroying or corrupting the original PDF files.
It guarantees that your LLM will only retrieve pure English context when answering questions!
